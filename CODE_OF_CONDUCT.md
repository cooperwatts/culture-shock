# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume good faith. Disagree about the work, not the person doing it.

## What we expect

This project is about helping colleagues connect with each other, which would be a poor look if the project itself were unpleasant to be around.

Contributors are expected to be respectful of differing viewpoints and experience levels, to give and accept criticism gracefully, and to focus on what is best for the project and the people who end up using it.

A few specifics worth naming, because this project touches workplace relationships and personal data:

Take privacy concerns seriously when someone raises them, even if the concern turns out to be unfounded. Dismissing them is corrosive.

Do not post real participant data in an issue, a pull request, or a log excerpt. Redact it. If you need to share a reproduction, construct one.

Remember that accessibility and inclusion requirements are requirements, not preferences. "We can add that later" is usually how it never gets added.

## What we will not accept

Harassment of any kind. Personal attacks. Sustained disruption. Publishing someone's private information without their permission. Sexualised language or imagery. Conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, and anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

If you see or experience something that breaches this, contact the maintainers privately through the email in `package.json` or through GitHub's private reporting. Reports will be handled discreetly.

Maintainers who do not uphold this in good faith can expect to be removed from the project.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that breach this document, and may temporarily or permanently ban anyone for behaviour they judge inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

We would rather have a conversation first. Most problems are a misunderstanding or a bad day, and are fixed by someone saying so.
